# Glossary

The words used in RANN's Roost and in this manual, with a short definition and the chapter that uses them most. The Canadian rules are summarized in a sentence or two; they are not tax or legal advice, and the amounts change from year to year.

## Words of the app {#app-terms}

@index: terms; definitions; vocabulary

- **Household**: one set of books, kept in its own encrypted folder on your computer. A family usually has one. See [Finding your way](basics).
- **Household member**: a person whose money, health or taxes the household follows, such as a parent or a child. A member does not need to sign in. See [Household members](members).
- **User**: someone who signs in, with their own login name, password and recovery key. A user can be linked to the household member they are. See [Users](users).
- **Administrator**: a user who can do everything, including users and settings, and who opens every shared account group.
- **Member**: as a role, a user who sees their own private accounts and the shared groups they are given.
- **Viewer**: a user who can only read, in the groups they are given.
- **Account group**: a set of accounts that share the same access. A shared group is opened by administrators and those given access; a private group by its owner and those they share it with. Each group is encrypted with its own key. See [Users](users).
- **Recovery key**: a long code given to each user, the only way back in when a password is forgotten. See [Privacy, data and security](privacy-data#recovery-key).
- **Base currency**: the currency in which totals and reports are shown, the Canadian dollar. Amounts in other currencies are converted at the Bank of Canada's rate. See [Rates and prices](rates).
- **Register**: the list of an account's transactions, with the running balance and the form to enter new ones. See [Accounts](accounts).
- **Opening balance**: what an account held on the date you started following it in the app. Every later balance is worked out from it.
- **Payee**: the person or business money goes to or comes from, such as a grocery store or an employer. See [Payees](payees).
- **Alias**: a piece of text that identifies a payee on bank statements, such as AMZN MKTP for Amazon, so imported lines get the right payee.
- **Category**: what money was spent on or where it came from, such as Groceries or Salary. Categories can have subcategories. Budgets and reports are built on them. See [Categories](categories).
- **Category rule**: an instruction that gives a category, and sometimes a payee, to imported transactions whose text or amount matches. See [Category rules](rules).
- **Split**: a transaction divided into several lines, each with its own category and amount, such as a store receipt with groceries and household items.
- **Transfer**: money moved between two of your own accounts, such as a credit card payment from chequing. It is neither an expense nor income.
- **Memo**: a free note on a transaction.
- **Cleared**: a transaction that has gone through the bank, marked c in the register.
- **Reconcile**: to check the app's transactions against the bank's statement, line by line, until the balances agree. Reconciled transactions are marked R and locked. See [Accounts](accounts).
- **Statement**: what the bank sends for a period, with its transactions and closing balance. It can be imported from a file (OFX, QFX, QBO or CSV) or entered from paper.
- **OFX, QFX, QBO and CSV**: file formats banks offer for downloading transactions. OFX, QFX and QBO carry the account number and the balance; CSV is a plain table whose columns you match once.
- **QIF**: the export format of Quicken, which the app can import with all your history. See [Accounts](accounts).
- **Vault**: the encrypted store inside the household where documents are kept. See [Documents](documents).
- **To review**: the tab of Documents where new documents, from imports, the watched folder or the phone, wait for you to check them before they touch the books.
- **Capture**: a receipt, bill, quick expense or odometer reading sent from RANN's Roost Mobile. See [RANN's Roost Mobile](phone-app).
- **Pairing**: linking a phone to the computer by scanning a QR code, once. See [Phones](phones).
- **Transfer folder**: a folder of your own cloud storage through which the phone sends captures when it is away from home.
- **Watched folder**: a folder whose new files, from a scanner or downloaded e-bills, are imported into Documents by themselves.
- **Archived**: kept for history but hidden from the lists used to enter new records.
- **Subscription**: a bill that renews by itself, such as a streaming service, followed with its yearly cost and the date to cancel by. See [Bills](bills).
- **Cash flow forecast**: the expected balance of an account in the coming days, from its bills and pay. See [Bills](bills).
- **Rollover**: in a budget, carrying the unspent or overspent amount into the next month. See [Budgets](budgets).
- **Set-aside**: money earmarked for a savings goal inside an account, by hand or on a schedule. See [Savings goals](goals).
- **Net worth**: everything owned (accounts, investments, the home, vehicles and other assets) minus everything owed. See [Reports](reports).
- **Reminder**: a notice of something coming due, shown in the banner at the top and as a computer notification. See [Finding your way](basics#reminders).

## Banking and borrowing {#banking}

@index: banking terms; loans; credit

- **Chequing account**: the everyday account that pay goes into and bills come out of.
- **GIC**: Guaranteed Investment Certificate, a deposit locked in for a term at a set rate. Term deposits are similar.
- **Line of credit**: a loan you can draw on and repay at will, up to a limit, paying interest only on what is used.
- **HELOC**: home equity line of credit, a line of credit secured by your home.
- **Mortgage**: a loan to buy a home, secured by it. See [Loans and mortgages](loans).
- **Amortization**: the total time a loan would take to be repaid with its regular payments, such as 25 years.
- **Term**: for a mortgage, the period its rate and conditions are set for, such as 5 years, after which it is renewed. The app reminds you when a term ends.
- **Accelerated payments**: weekly or bi-weekly payments set so that a year's payments add up to more than twelve monthly ones, which shortens the loan.
- **Prepayment**: an extra payment on a loan's principal, which reduces the interest to come.
- **Principal**: the amount borrowed or still owed, without the interest.
- **Pre-authorized debit**: a payment the payee takes from your account on the due date, with your permission.
- **Exchange rate**: the price of one currency in another. The app uses the Bank of Canada's daily rate, or one you enter. See [Rates and prices](rates).

## Registered plans {#registered-plans}

@index: registered plans; tax-sheltered accounts; contribution room

See [Registered plans](plans) for how the app follows each plan.

- **RRSP**: Registered Retirement Savings Plan. Contributions are deducted from your income, up to your deduction limit; money grows untaxed and is taxed when withdrawn. Contributions made in the first 60 days of a year can be deducted for the year before. An RRSP must be converted, usually into a RRIF, by December 31 of the year you turn 71. The CRA charges 1 % a month on over-contributions above a $2,000 buffer.
- **Spousal RRSP**: an RRSP that one spouse contributes to and deducts, but that belongs to the other spouse, to share retirement income.
- **RRIF**: Registered Retirement Income Fund, what an RRSP usually becomes. A minimum must be withdrawn each year after the first, a percentage of its value that grows with age; withdrawals are taxed.
- **TFSA**: Tax-Free Savings Account. Contributions are not deducted, but growth and withdrawals are tax-free. Room builds up each year from age 18; amounts withdrawn are added back to the room the next year. Over-contributions are charged 1 % a month.
- **FHSA**: First Home Savings Account. Contributions are deducted like an RRSP's and withdrawals for a first home are tax-free, like a TFSA's. Up to $8,000 a year and $40,000 in all.
- **RESP**: Registered Education Savings Plan, to save for a child's education after high school. Contributions are not deducted; grants are added; the growth and grants are taxed in the student's hands when paid out. Contributions are limited to $50,000 per child over their life.
- **CESG**: Canada Education Savings Grant, the federal grant added to an RESP: 20 % of the first $2,500 contributed each year for a child, up to $7,200 in all.
- **Canada Learning Bond**: a federal amount paid into the RESP of a child from a low-income family, without a contribution.
- **QESI**: Quebec education savings incentive, the Quebec grant added to the RESP of a child living in Quebec.
- **BCTESG**: B.C. Training and Education Savings Grant, paid once into the RESP of a child living in British Columbia, from age 6, without a contribution.
- **LIRA**: Locked-in Retirement Account, holding pension money moved from an employer's plan; it cannot be withdrawn freely. Its rules depend on the province or territory, or federal law, that governs the pension.
- **LIF**: Life Income Fund, what a LIRA becomes to pay an income, with a minimum and a maximum withdrawal each year set by the governing jurisdiction.
- **Pension plan**: an employer's retirement plan, defined benefit or defined contribution.
- **Contribution room**: how much can still be put into a plan this year without penalty, worked out from the figures on your notice of assessment.
- **Deduction limit**: for an RRSP, the most you can deduct for a year, shown on your notice of assessment.

## Investing {#investing}

@index: investing terms; stocks; funds; capital gains

See [Investments](investments).

- **Security**: anything bought and sold as units on a market or from a fund company: a share, an ETF, a mutual fund, a bond.
- **ETF**: Exchange-Traded Fund, a fund bought and sold on the stock market like a share.
- **Holding**: a security you own in an account, with its number of units.
- **Non-registered account**: an ordinary investment account (brokerage), outside any registered plan, whose income and gains are taxed each year.
- **ACB**: Adjusted Cost Base, what your units cost you for tax purposes, including commissions, averaged over all purchases. In the app it is pooled across all non-registered accounts with the same owners, in Canadian dollars at each transaction's date.
- **Capital gain**: the selling price minus the ACB and selling costs. Half of a capital gain is generally taxable; a capital loss can offset gains.
- **Superficial loss**: a loss on a sale when the same security was bought within 30 days before or after it. Such a loss may be denied and added to the cost of the new units. The app flags possible ones.
- **Return of capital**: money a fund pays back from your own investment. It is not income; it lowers the ACB.
- **DRIP**: Dividend Reinvestment Plan, where income is used to buy more units instead of being paid in cash.
- **Asset allocation**: how investments are spread between kinds, such as Canadian stocks, foreign stocks and bonds, compared with a target.
- **Rebalancing**: buying and selling to bring the allocation back to its target.
- **Time-weighted return**: how the investments themselves performed, whatever the timing of deposits and withdrawals.
- **Personal rate of return**: the money-weighted return, which also counts when your money went in and out.
- **Crypto-asset**: a digital asset such as bitcoin, held in a wallet or on an exchange.
- **Watch-only wallet**: a Bitcoin wallet followed by its public address or extended public key (xpub, ypub, zpub), which shows the balance but can never spend. Never enter a private key or a seed phrase.
- **Spot price**: the current market price of a precious metal, per ounce or gram.
- **Foreign exchange gain**: the gain or loss from the change in an exchange rate on foreign currency you held. For individuals, the first $200 of net gain in a year is not taxed.

## Taxes and slips {#taxes}

@index: tax terms; tax slips; T4; Relevé 1

See [Taxes](taxes). In Quebec, provincial slips are called Relevés (RL).

- **CRA**: the Canada Revenue Agency, which collects federal income tax, and provincial income tax outside Quebec.
- **Revenu Québec**: the Quebec tax authority, which collects Quebec income tax and the QST.
- **Notice of assessment**: the CRA's answer to your return, which gives your RRSP deduction limit and TFSA and FHSA figures.
- **T4**: the slip of employment income and deductions from an employer. In Quebec, the RL-1 goes with it.
- **T4A**: a slip of pension, retiring allowance and other income. T4A(P) is for CPP or QPP benefits and T4A(OAS) for Old Age Security.
- **T4E**: the slip of Employment Insurance benefits.
- **T4RSP and T4RIF**: the slips of RRSP and RRIF withdrawals. RL-2 is the Quebec slip of retirement income.
- **T4FHSA**: the FHSA slip.
- **T5**: the slip of investment income (interest, dividends) from a non-registered account. In Quebec, the RL-3.
- **T3**: the slip of income from a trust, such as a mutual fund or ETF. In Quebec, the RL-16.
- **T5008**: the slip of securities sold, used to work out capital gains. In Quebec, the RL-18.
- **T2202**: the slip of tuition fees. In Quebec, the RL-8.
- **RL-6 and RL-24**: Quebec slips of parental insurance benefits and of child care expenses.
- **RRSP contribution receipt**: the receipt for RRSP contributions, needed to deduct them.
- **Official donation receipt**: the receipt a registered charity gives for a gift, with its registration number (nine digits, RR and four digits). See [Taxes](taxes).
- **Tax instalments**: tax paid ahead, on March 15, June 15, September 15 and December 15, when not enough is withheld at source. They are due when the tax owing at filing is more than $3,000 ($1,800 in Quebec) this year and in either of the two years before.
- **GST, HST, QST and PST**: sales taxes. The GST is federal; the HST combines it with the provincial tax in some provinces; the QST is Quebec's; a PST is charged in some other provinces (in Manitoba it is called the retail sales tax, RST). Their rates, by date and province, are in [Rates and rules](rates-rules).
- **Medical expense tax credit**: a credit for medical expenses paid in any 12 consecutive months ending in the year, above a threshold of 3 % of net income (or a set amount if lower). A couple usually claims them together on one return. See [Medical claims](medical).
- **CPP and QPP**: the Canada Pension Plan and the Quebec Pension Plan, paid from work income and paying a retirement pension.
- **OAS**: Old Age Security, the federal pension from age 65.
- **EI**: Employment Insurance.
- **Tax bracket**: a band of taxable income taxed at one rate; each dollar is taxed at the rate of the band it falls in. See [Estimate](taxes#estimate).
- **Marginal rate**: the tax on one more dollar of income, federal and provincial together. A deduction such as an RRSP contribution saves roughly this rate.
- **Average rate**: the total income tax as a share of total income.
- **Net income and taxable income**: total income less deductions (line 23600), and net income less some further deductions (line 26000). Many credits and benefits depend on net income.
- **Basic personal amount**: the income on which everyone pays no tax, given as a non-refundable credit. The federal one is smaller at high incomes.
- **Non-refundable credit**: an amount (basic personal, age, CPP, EI, medical...) multiplied by the lowest tax rate and taken off the tax. It cannot bring the tax below zero.
- **Dividend tax credit**: a credit for the corporate tax already paid on dividends from Canadian companies, worked out on the grossed-up (taxable) amount.
- **Quebec abatement**: a reduction of 16.5 % of the federal tax for Quebec residents, who pay more Quebec tax in its place.
- **Surtax**: an extra tax on the provincial tax above a threshold (Ontario).
- **Ontario Health Premium**: an amount added to Ontario tax, by tiers of taxable income, up to $900.
- **Tuition tax credit**: a non-refundable credit on eligible tuition fees (T2202, RL-8). The student uses it first; up to $5,000 of the year's fees can go to a spouse, parent or grandparent (in Quebec, a parent or grandparent), and the rest is carried forward. See [Tuition and carry-forwards](taxes#estimate-carry-forward).
- **Carry-forward**: an amount not used in its year that can be claimed in a later year: unused tuition, donations (up to five years), net capital losses, unused RRSP contributions. The notice of assessment gives most of them.

## Health and family {#health-family}

@index: health terms; insurance; family money

- **Explanation of benefits**: the statement an insurer sends after a claim, showing what was paid and what was not. See [Medical claims](medical).
- **Dependant**: a person you support, such as a child or a parent, who may give you tax credits.
- **Shared expense**: a cost paid by one person for several, to be divided among them. See [Family money](family).
- **Allowance**: money given regularly to a child. See [Family money](family).

## Emergency and estate {#estate}

@index: estate terms; will; executor

See [Emergency and estate](estate).

- **Will**: the document that says who inherits and who settles the estate.
- **Executor**: the person named in a will to settle the estate.
- **Liquidator**: in Quebec, the person who settles the estate, the equivalent of an executor.
- **Power of attorney for property**: a document naming someone to manage your money if you cannot.
- **Protection mandate**: in Quebec, the document naming someone to look after you and your property if you become unable to; elsewhere, the power of attorney for personal care or the health care directive covers the person.
- **Beneficiary**: the person named to receive a plan, a policy or a share of an estate.
