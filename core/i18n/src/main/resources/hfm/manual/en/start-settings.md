# Getting started with settings

The **Settings** group of the menu holds what the rest of the app relies on: the people, who signs in, the categories, the backups and the locking. A few minutes here at the start saves corrections later. This chapter says what to set first and why; each step links to the full chapter.

## Before you start {#before}

You have created the household and saved your recovery key (see [Quick start](quick-start)). You are signed in as its first administrator. The Settings group starts folded in the menu on the left: click **Settings** to open it.

## The first settings, in order {#first-settings}

### 1. Check the province and add the people {#step-members}

Why first: accounts, plans, medical claims and tax slips all belong to a person, and the province decides bank holidays, default categories, RESP grants and tax forms.

1. Open **Household members**.
2. Check the **Province or territory** at the top.
3. Click **Add** for each person: yourself, your spouse or partner, the children, any other dependant.
4. For each, choose the **Relationship** and enter the **Date of birth (YYYY-MM-DD)**. The date is needed for TFSA room, RRIF minimums and RESP grants.
5. For someone who lives in another province, such as a student away at school, choose **Lives in**.

Details: [Household members](members).

### 2. Move the backups to a safe place {#step-backups}

Why now: a new household is already backed up every day, but into a folder beside it on the same disk. If the disk fails, both are lost.

1. Open **Backups**.
2. Click **Choose folder…** and pick an external drive, a network drive or a cloud folder (OneDrive, Google Drive, Dropbox).
3. Leave **Automatic backups** at **Every day**, and **Versions to keep** at 10 or more.
4. Click **Back up now** and wait for "Backup saved and checked".

Details: [Backups](backups).

### 3. Choose when the household locks {#step-security}

Why: an open household shows everything to whoever sits at the computer.

1. Open **Security**.
2. Choose **Lock after inactivity**: 5 minutes on a shared computer, longer on your own.
3. Check that your recovery key is printed and stored away from the computer.

Details: [Security](security).

### 4. Add the other people who sign in {#step-users}

Why: each person should have their own password, so the activity log says who did what and private accounts stay private.

1. Open **Users**.
2. Click **Add a user** for your spouse or partner, or a teenager who will capture receipts.
3. Choose the **Role**: Administrator for a partner who manages everything with you, Member for most others, Viewer for someone who should only look.
4. Link each user to their person in **This user is the household member**.
5. Hand each new user their recovery key, to print and keep.
6. On the **Access** tab, choose what each person can open in each account group.

Details: [Users](users).

### 5. Make the app comfortable {#step-display}

1. Open **Display and accessibility**.
2. Choose the **Colours** and the **Text size** that suit you.
3. Use **English** or **Français** at the top of the window to choose the language.
4. If you prefer, put the menu at the top with **Menu at the top**.

These choices are kept on this computer. Details: [Display and accessibility](display).

### 6. Look over the categories {#step-categories}

Why: categories feed budgets, reports and the tax package. The default list covers most Canadian households.

1. Open **Categories**.
2. Archive the categories you will never use (tick **Archived (hidden from lists)** and **Save**).
3. Add any you miss, such as a category for a hobby or a side business.
4. Check the **Tax treatment** of the categories you claim, such as child care, donations or tuition.

Details: [Categories](categories).

### 7. After the first import: payees and rules {#step-rules}

Rules and payee aliases are easiest to set up once you have imported a first statement and can see how your bank writes names.

1. Import a statement into an account (see [Accounts](accounts)).
2. On **Payees**, give the odd bank names an alias, so they come in clean next time.
3. On **Category rules**, add a rule for each regular line that came in without a category, such as "HYDRO-QUEBEC" → Electricity. Add specific rules before general ones.

Details: [Payees](payees) and [Category rules](rules).

## Later, when you need them {#later}

- [Institutions](institutions): your banks' phone numbers, websites, and institution and transit numbers.
- [Rates and prices](rates): exchange rates for foreign currency accounts (automatic), and optional market prices for stocks, crypto-assets and precious metals.
- [Rates and rules](rates-rules): every tax rate, plan limit, threshold and lead time the app applies, with its date and province. The official values are built in; an administrator can enter a new figure, such as next year's TFSA limit, as soon as it is announced.
- [Phones](phones): pair RANN's Roost Mobile to capture receipts.
- [AI reading](ai): let Claude read hard documents with your own Anthropic key.
- [About](about): your version, and update checks on Linux packages.

> Tip: The **Getting started** card on the [Dashboard](dashboard) walks through the people, accounts, bills, a first statement and the phone, and ticks each step off as you do it.
