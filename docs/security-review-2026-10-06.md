# Security review of the 2026-10-06 round

Everything built on 2026-10-06 (commits `d72479f` to `719fb7e`): calendars from the phone and .ics import (CSY-01 to CSY-05), calendar views, schedules and activities (CAL-07 to CAL-11), seasonal checklists and rebates (SEA-01 to SEA-05), trips, places, fuel and charging and the vehicle forecast (TRP-01 to TRP-10), utilities, tanks, hours worked, chores and volunteer hours (UTL-01, UTL-02, HRS-01, CHO-01, VOL-01). Same standard as the Phase 3, 4 and 5 reviews: what leaves the device is only what the user asked to send; outside input (an .ics file, what a phone sends) cannot use up memory, time or the file system; core.db, which every user of the household can read, holds no amounts, names of private records, typed text, places or coordinates; and each user sees only the groups they may open (HH-11), with the permission checked in the service, not only in the screens.

## Findings and fixes

| # | Severity | Finding | Fixed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Medium | **Another user's phone could be redirected.** The Phones screen lists every phone of the household, and any user, a viewer included, could edit another user's phone: its name and the account group its captures go to. `SyncService.update`, `revoke` and `forget` checked nothing. Since this round the phone's group also receives its trips, the places saved on it with their coordinates, volunteer hours and the other log entries: a user could have moved another person's phone from their private group to a group they read, and from then on received that person's trips and places. The group was not even checked to be one the owner may add to. (Present since Phase 2; its weight came with this round.) | `update` is for the phone's owner only, and only to a group the owner may add to; `revoke` and `forget` for its owner or an administrator (an administrator can still take a lost phone off). The Phones screen shows Edit on one's own phones only, and Remove or Forget on one's own, or on all for an administrator. The group hint now says that everyone who can open the group sees what the phone sends, and that only its owner can change it (EN and FR). `MultiUserTest`. |
| 2 | Medium | **A calendar made less visible kept its past items visible.** The phone sends only the coming days, and the desktop replaced a calendar's items from the snapshot's first day on. When the owner changed a calendar from shared to busy only, or to private, the items already past kept their titles and places, and the calendar its name, in the group others read, for up to a year (the pruning period): the owner's choice applied only to what was still to come. | When a snapshot makes a calendar less visible, its past items are placed again with it: none stays more visible than the calendar now is (busy only: times alone for others; private: nothing left where others can read, the owner keeps them in full). `BroughtInCalendarTest`. |
| 3 | Low | **.ics files: repeats from far back.** `ICalendar.occurrences` went through every day, week, month or year from an event's start to the window: an event starting in the year 1 and repeating daily took some 740,000 steps, and a 5 MB file holds about 5,000 such events, several billion steps on the screen's thread. Each event could also carry hundreds of thousands of dates left out (EXDATE), each written as a cancelled date, and the expanded repeats could make up to 1.25 million single appointments. | Without a COUNT, the periods before the window are skipped; the periods gone through are taken from one budget per file (200,000), and a repeat cut short by it is named in the notes ("ics.limit", EN and FR); at most 1,000 dates left out per event and 5,000 single dates per file. `ICalendarTest`, `BroughtInCalendarTest` (300 runaway repeats import in a fraction of a second). Nothing referenced by a file (ATTACH, URL, TZURL) is fetched; unknown time zones are taken as the computer's. |
| 4 | Low | **Numbers from the phone with an exponent.** Fill-up quantities and costs, meter and tank readings, the cost of a task ticked in the seasonal checklist and a quick expense's amount (the last since Phase 2) were read with `toBigDecimal`, which accepts `1E999999999`; writing it out (`toPlainString`) or scaling it to cents makes a billion-digit number, enough to exhaust the desktop's memory. Only the paired phone can send it, but one request was enough to bring the program down. | Every decimal from a phone goes through `phoneDecimal`: at most 12 digits before the point and 6 after, refused cleanly otherwise (the phone shows the reason). `TripsOnPhoneTest`, `TrackerSyncTest`. |
| 5 | Low | **Text from the phone without a length.** Meter and tank notes, the description of hours, the organization and activity of volunteer hours, a seasonal task's note, and the places typed at a trip's ends were stored as sent. | Cut like the rest of the phone's text: 500 characters, 120 for names (`phoneText`). `TripsOnPhoneTest`, `TrackerSyncTest`. |
| 6 | Low | **Ids a phone names on a trip.** The trailer and the driver of a trip from the phone were stored without checking that they were a trailer and a member of the household the user can see. | A trailer must be a visible asset of the kind trailer, a driver a member; otherwise the trip is refused with the reason. `TripsOnPhoneTest`. |
| 7 | Low | **Capture-only phones could rename places.** A place sent again from the phone renamed the household's saved place, with the right to add only. | Renaming a known place needs the right to change in its group; a new place still needs only the right to add. `TripsOnPhoneTest`. |
| 8 | Low | **Hours by id.** `WorkHoursService.save` looked for an existing entry among the chosen client's hours only, so the id of another client's hours in the same group would have changed them (the query's upsert) with the right to add alone. Not reachable from the phone or the screens, which never pass such an id. | The id is looked up among all hours; one of another client is refused. `TrackersTest`. |
| 9 | Low | **Removing a calendar where one can no longer add.** Removing a brought-in calendar (on the computer, or because the phone stopped bringing it in) and moving it to another group deleted it only from groups the owner may add to: a copy left in a group whose access had been lowered to view stayed readable by others. An item id of any length was stored. | The owner's calendar is deleted from every group that holds it; event ids over 64 characters are left out (the phone's are numbers). `BroughtInCalendarTest`. |
| 10 | Low | **Privacy policy and Data safety draft.** The policy did not say that places and trips are kept in the account group the phone sends to, where the household members who can open it see them and their phones receive the places; the Data safety draft's list of what goes to the phone left out schedules, places (with coordinates) and trailers. | Both say so (`website/privacy-policy-en.md`, `-fr.md`, plain text ready for Google Sites; `docs/store/google-play.md`), and that calendar items brought in never go to any phone. |

`AuditPrivacyTest` now also covers this round: a calendar brought in with a private item, an .ics import, a school schedule, an activity with a driver and a cost that became a transaction, a place with coordinates and an address, a trip with notes and passengers, a fill-up, a meter and a reading, a tank and a delivery paid from an account, a client with hours turned into an invoice, a chore ticked and volunteer hours. None of the titles, places, coordinates, amounts, notes or names reaches the audit log; the only new entry is the .ics import, with its counts.

## Checked and kept

- **Calendars brought in (CSY-01 to CSY-04).** A snapshot is stored only for the phone's own user: the desktop opens a request only from a paired, unrevoked phone whose owner is signed in (otherwise `OwnerAwayException`), and a calendar's id is a hash of the phone's id and its own key, so one phone can neither reach another phone's calendars nor another user's. Private items and the details of busy-only ones are kept only in the owner's private group (made for them if needed); the group for others holds shared items in full and busy-only ones as times alone, with the calendar's name only when the whole calendar is shared. A single item's own choice wins over its calendar's on both sides. An older snapshot changes nothing; a removal deletes the calendar and its items. At most 40 calendars per request, 1,500 items per calendar, 300 characters per text. Brought-in items appear only in the calendar views: not in the global search, reports, exports or the phone's reference data (whose events are the computer's own appointments, from the groups the phone's user can see). Only the owner manages their calendars (`calendars`, `remove` and `setOthersGroup` start from their own private group).
- **The phone's calendar reading (CSY-01).** `READ_CALENDAR` is asked only when the user turns on Calendars on this phone, after the app says why; only visible calendars that the user ticked are read, for the days chosen, never descriptions, guests or reminders, and nothing is written to them. Turning it off sends a removal of each calendar at the next transfer. The choices, the snapshots waiting and the per-item visibilities are in the phone's encrypted store.
- **Location (TRP-01, TRP-02).** `ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION` and `ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION` only; no `ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION` in any flavour's merged manifest, no foreground service of the location type, no Play services: `OneFix` asks Android's own location service for one fix when the trip form opens (if already allowed) or when the user taps Use my location, and the permission is asked only then. The fix is matched to saved places on the phone; nothing is sent to a map service. The trip in progress, the places saved on the phone and the last odometers are kept with `PhoneStore.keep`, encrypted with the phone's Keystore key. Places reach only phones whose user can see their group (the reference data is built for the phone's own user while signed in). Coordinates are never in the audit log; on the computer they are shown in Places only, and in a trip only when the user left an end unnamed (see below).
- **Exported components and notifications.** The manifest gains only the calendar and location permissions; the exported main activity and its filters, the non-exported receiver and FileProvider are unchanged. No new notification: schedules, brought-in items, seasonal tasks and log entries are shown in the app only, behind its PIN.
- **Privacy between users (HH-11) and permissions.** New records are in each group's ledger: brought-in calendars, schedules and their exceptions, activities (drivers, cost), seasonal ticks (service logs), rebates, places, trips, fill-ups, meters, tanks, clients and hours, chores and volunteer hours. Every list reads only the groups the user can open; every write checks the group's level in the service: editors for schedules, activities, rebates, meters, tanks, clients, chores, deliveries, deleting anything; the right to add for readings, hours, chore ticks, volunteer hours, fill-ups, trips, new places and seasonal ticks. A phone's entries are always new records (a trip or fill-up sent again is recognized by its id and changes nothing). The vehicle budget lines written to core.db count only vehicles of shared groups.
- **Phone entries.** Dates are parsed and refused when invalid, minutes limited to a day, times to HH:MM, meters, tanks, clients, tasks, chores, members and vehicles must be ones the user can see, quantities positive; each failure goes back to the phone with its reason, and the others go on.
- **Files written.** The seasonal checklist prints through `PrintFiles` (removed after printing) and is saved as a PDF only where the user chooses, like a report; it holds task names and dates, no amounts.

## Accepted, with reasons

- **An unnamed trip end keeps its coordinates** as the trip's origin or destination (to five decimals), in the trip log and the CRA logbook. The user sees them on the phone before saving and can name the place instead; a logbook needs a destination, and coordinates are the user's own record of it.
- **Busy-only entries** show others the owner's name and the times, as CSY-03 asks.
- **Children's chores** can be ticked by anyone who may add in the chore's group (a child on their own phone), each tick earning its amount. A parent sees each tick, from which phone, and pays them; a tick not paid can be removed.
- **An activity's cost** becomes a transaction whose payee is the activity's place or title, in the account the user chooses: the user decides where it is recorded.
- **Calendars are read in the hourly transfer** on the phone too, not only when the app is open: the transfers are what brings changes to the computer (CSY-02), the policy says what is read and how to stop it.
- **A phone's snapshot time** (`takenAtMillis`) is the phone's own: a paired phone can only order its own calendars' snapshots, and only the pair key can produce one.
- **The vehicle forecast** follows every trip the user can see, private ones included; budget lines are only proposed, and setting a budget is the user's choice (as for budget suggestions in Phase 5).
- **Decompression and request size** stay bounded only by the request (200 MB) once a phone is authenticated, as in Phase 5; this round's lists are each capped (40 calendars, 50 entries of each kind per request).

## Tests

New or extended: `MultiUserTest` (another user's phone), `BroughtInCalendarTest` (narrowed calendar, overlong ids, runaway .ics repeats), `ICalendarTest` (repeats from long ago, the shared budget, dates left out), `TripsOnPhoneTest` (capture-only rename, numbers with exponents, unknown trailer and driver, long texts), `TrackerSyncTest` (numbers and texts from the phone), `TrackersTest` (hours by id), `AuditPrivacyTest` (this round's records).

## Follow-ups (exit check)

The exit check of the round (`3fc7897`) listed further security observations. All are fixed, without any change to the database schema; `TripsOnPhoneTest` covers them.

| # | Observation | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Trips stored in the working group.** A trip, from the phone or the computer, went to the phone's (or the screen's) group with the permission checked there, not on the vehicle's: a user who may only view a vehicle could add trips, and so odometer readings, to it; an administrator's phone, which stores in the shared group, put the trips of a car kept in a private group where others read. | Fixed. A trip in a vehicle is kept in the vehicle's group, and refused unless the user may add there (`TripService.store`). Fill-ups and odometer readings already went to the vehicle's group with that check. |
| 2 | **A deleted place came back** when a phone that still had it renamed it. | Fixed. Deleting a place remembers its id (`place.deleted.<id>` in the household settings: a random id, no name or coordinates), and a phone's change to it is refused. An archived place renamed from a phone stays archived. |
| 3 | **Seasonal ticks for what the user may only view.** The phone's checklist offered the tasks of vehicles and assets in groups the user may only view; the tick was then refused by the service log. | Fixed. The phone's list leaves them out (`SeasonalChecklistService.mayTick`), and `record` refuses them before anything else. Changing a service or fill-up already saved now needs the right to change (`saveService`, `saveFuel`), adding one the right to add, as before. |
| 4 | **Saved before the sync record.** A fill-up was stored in the ledger, then noted as received in core.db; the two are different databases, so one transaction cannot hold both, and a stop in between would have stored it twice when the phone sent it again. | Fixed by the phone's id: a fill-up is kept under the id the phone gave it, and one already stored under that id is acknowledged without storing it again. A seasonal tick is already recorded at most once per task and date (exit check). Trips already kept the phone's id. |
| 5 | **Double tap on the phone's fuel form.** Two taps on Save queued two fill-ups. | Fixed. Save is disabled while saving, the form keeps one id, and the phone's queue keeps one entry per id. |
