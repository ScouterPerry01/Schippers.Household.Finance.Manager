# Security review of the 2026-10-07 round

Everything built on 2026-10-07 (commits `9663632` to `7126811`): trips with stops, breaks, notes and photos, address lookup and stations nearby (TRP-11 to TRP-19), the phone's lock time (SEC-09), calendar sync both ways (CSY-06), the agenda icon (CAL-12), the document viewer and itemizing by hand (DOC-01, DOC-02), bill classification, statements, meter readings, bills from captured documents, Business bills, payments in part and instalments (BILL-13 to BILL-25), column headings (NAV-04), and the Help menu, About and Walk-Me guides (HLP-01 to HLP-04). Same standard as the earlier reviews: what leaves the device is only what the user asked to send; outside input (a phone, a PDF or photo, an AI answer, OpenStreetMap) cannot use up memory, time or the file system; core.db, which every user of the household can read, holds no amounts, names of private records, typed text, account numbers, places or coordinates; and each user sees only the groups they may open (HH-11), with the permission checked in the service, not only in the screens. No schema change was needed (one new query); FORMAT stays as it is; no permission was added.

## Findings and fixes

| # | Severity | Finding | Fixed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Medium | **Trip photos and notes filed in the phone's group, not the trip's.** Since the 2026-10-06 follow-up a trip is kept with its vehicle, but the photos, notes and voice notes taken during it (TRP-16) were still imported into the group the phone sends to. An administrator's phone sends to the shared group: the photos of a trip in a car kept in a private group, labelled with the trip, landed where the whole household reads them. | A capture that names a trip is kept in the trip's group when the trip is already on the computer, else in its vehicle's group (the phone now sends the vehicle with each photo or note), when the user may add there; otherwise the phone's group, as before. Other captures are unchanged. `TripStopsOnPhoneTest`. |
| 2 | Low | **PDF pages drawn at any size.** The document viewer (150 DPI), reading a scanned PDF (200 DPI), the pages for an AI reading (150 DPI) and previews drew each page at a fixed resolution whatever its size. A PDF page can be 200 inches square (14,400 points): 30,000 pixels a side, 3.6 GB at 150 DPI. A PDF from a phone, an import or an email could stop the program for lack of memory as soon as it was opened. | Pages are drawn at the chosen resolution, or less so that their longer side stays within 2,400 pixels (viewer, AI) or 4,000 (reading); images inside a page are read only as finely as the page is drawn. A letter page is drawn exactly as before. `UntrustedFilesTest`. Photos were already reduced to 3,200 pixels while decoding. |
| 3 | Low | **Numbers with huge exponents in AI answers.** An AI answer is outside input (the document read can try to steer it). `1E999999999` is valid JSON; rounding it to the cent or writing it out (a meter reading kept with the document, an instalment amount, and since Phase 5 a total, a tax or a statement line) makes a billion-digit number. | Every number of an answer is taken only with at most 12 digits before the point and 10 after (`ReadNumbers`), in the bill, receipt, pay stub, statement and investment readings; the rest are left out like an unreadable field. `AiTypesTest`. |
| 4 | Low | **Instalments without a limit.** A statement could list any number of instalments (from an AI answer or the on-device reading of a long document), each becoming a due date with reminders, proposed again every year for up to 50 years. | At most 60 instalments per statement: more is refused by the service ("error.billInstalments", EN and FR), and a reading keeps the first 60. `BillInstalmentsTest`, `AiTypesTest`. |
| 5 | Low | **A trip half saved.** A trip and its stops were written one after the other, outside a transaction, under the ids the phone gave its stops. A stop id sent twice, or already used by another trip of the group, failed on the database's key after the trip itself was written: the trip stayed without its later stops, and the phone's next transfer was taken as already received. | A trip and its stops are written in one transaction; a stop id given twice, or already another trip's, gets a new one. `TripStopsOnPhoneTest`. |
| 6 | Low | **"At once" could wait a minute after a permission request.** SEC-09 lets the app come back from a screen it opened itself (the scanner, a picker) without the PIN for a minute. The mark was set when the screen was asked for and cleared only when the app came back to the front from being stopped. A permission request is a dialog that does not stop the app, and a screen that failed to open never did: the mark stayed, and the next time the user left the app, coming back within a minute did not ask for the PIN even with "At once" chosen. | The time away counts as spent in the app's own screen only when the app was left within 5 seconds of opening it, and the mark goes when the app is back without having been left (`LockWatch`, plain Kotlin in core/sync). `PhoneLockTest`. |
| 7 | Low | **The recent apps screen showed the last screen.** The PIN is asked when the app comes back, but Android's list of recent apps kept a picture of the app as it was left (amounts, the agenda, a trip), even with "At once" chosen. | On Android 13 and later the app gives no picture to the recent apps screen (`setRecentsScreenshotEnabled(false)`); screenshots stay possible (the manual's phone pictures are taken that way). Android 12 and earlier have no such switch short of blocking screenshots altogether. |
| 8 | Low | **An address looked up without a tap.** With "Look up addresses" on, opening "Add a station by hand" sent the position to Google's geocoder on its own, while Settings says the lookup happens "only when you tap it". | The form takes the position, and the address is looked up only when the user taps Look up the address. The privacy policy names both places the button appears (EN and FR). |
| 9 | Low | **Overpass answers without an overall time limit.** The request to OpenStreetMap had a connection and a read timeout, but the read timeout applies to each read: a server sending a byte every few seconds kept the search going for as long as it liked. | An answer must arrive within 45 seconds in all, as well as within 1 MB (`NearbyStations.readAnswer`). `NearbyStationsTest`, which also feeds the parser hostile answers: nesting 100,000 deep, numbers out of range, a megabyte-long name, wrong types and 5,000 stations. |
| 10 | Low | **Items the app wrote could be brought back in.** Bringing calendars in left out the events whose ids the app remembered writing. Events it wrote but no longer follows (past ones, after 30 days; all of them after the app's data was erased or the app reinstalled) in an account calendar the user also brings in would have come back to the computer as the user's own items. | Reading the phone's calendars leaves out every event marked with the app's package (`CUSTOM_APP_PACKAGE`), which every written event carries, whether still followed or not; the agenda's view of the phone's calendars likewise. |
| 11 | Low | **A photo's orientation tag read outside the file.** Reading the EXIF orientation of a JPEG followed an offset taken from the file without checking it, so a crafted offset could make it read before the start of the array (an exception, caught by the callers). | The offset is read as unsigned and checked against the file's length. `UntrustedFilesTest`. |

The privacy policy also says now that Android does not remove what the app wrote into a calendar when the app is uninstalled, so "Both ways" should be turned off first (EN and FR).

`AuditPrivacyTest` now also covers this round: a Business bill with its account number (revealed with the password, then changed), a statement with its number, notes and meter readings, a payment in part then the rest and one undone, a tax bill's instalments and its statement deleted, a bill list renamed, a trip with positions, addresses, a stop with notes and a break, and a place with its address. None of the names, numbers, amounts, readings, addresses, coordinates or notes reaches the audit log; revealing the account number is logged, without it.

## Checked and kept

- **Address lookup (TRP-17).** Off by default; the geocoder is called only from Look up the address, shown only with the option on and a position taken; it receives that fix's coordinates (an address needs them), nothing else, with a 15-second limit. Google's own service, through Android, handles the request: no key, no URL of ours. The policy and the Data safety draft (item 7) say so.
- **Stations nearby (TRP-18).** Off by default; with it off the screen only explains and offers to turn it on, and no request is made. Only the position rounded to two decimals (about a kilometre) is sent, in a fixed query; distances are worked out on the phone. HTTPS only (the endpoint is checked), redirects not followed, 10-second connection and 30-second read timeouts, now 45 seconds and 1 MB in all, one retry when Overpass is busy. The User-Agent names the app and its version only. The answer is read defensively: each element on its own, positions within range, ids as numbers, texts cut to 120 characters, the closest 40 kept. A station saved as a place goes through the same checks as any place from the phone.
- **Trips from the phone (TRP-11 to TRP-16).** The 2026-10-06 fix stands: a trip is kept with its vehicle, refused unless the user may add there. Stops: at most 100, times parsed and refused when invalid, a break's end after its start, odometers rising from the start through each stop to the arrival, places by id only among the places the user can see, texts cut (120, 200 for addresses, 500 for notes), coordinates kept only in range and in pairs, ids over 64 characters replaced. A photo's trip and stop ids are links only (64 characters at most); a photo naming an unknown trip waits in the inbox. Coordinates and addresses are never in the audit log (test above).
- **Calendar writing (CSY-06).** What is written comes only from the reference data built for the phone's own user while signed in (the groups they can see). In every target, phone-only or an account's, a medical appointment is "Health appointment" with no place and no drivers, and a bill is "Bill due" with its name, never an amount; descriptions hold only who it is for, the drivers and the footer, never notes. The app writes only into the phone-only calendar it made or the account calendar the user picked, checked each time to still be writable; a change of calendar removes everything first. Turning it off, unpairing, the desktop removing the phone and erasing the app after a forgotten PIN remove every event it wrote (by id, then by the app's package) and the phone-only calendar; without the permission nothing is removed and it is tried again. `WRITE_CALENDAR` is asked only when the user picks Both ways, after the app says why. Written events are never sent to the computer (finding 10).
- **Lock time (SEC-09).** The choice is read on every return; values other than 0, 1, 5 and 15 count as one minute. The PIN screen is the start state, so a new process (after the app was killed or the phone restarted) and a configuration change both ask again. Away time is measured on the elapsed-time clock, which changing the phone's clock does not move. The own-screen rule never allows more than a minute, or the longer time chosen (finding 6). The lock time can be changed only from Settings, behind the PIN.
- **Bills (BILL-13 to BILL-25).** The account number with the company is kept in the bill's ledger; the `Bill` the screens get holds it masked ("•••• 6789"); the full one comes only through `revealAccountNumber`, after the user's password (`RevealGuard`: a wait after three wrong ones, failures logged without the password), and the edit form shows the masked one with Show and Change. It is used in full only to match a captured bill, never shown. It is not in the audit log, the phone's reference data (bills carry name, date and the amount still due), report exports or logs. Statements, readings, payments and instalments: viewing needs only access to the group; recording or deleting a statement, setting an amount, skipping and undoing a payment need the right to change; paying needs the right to add, as since Phase 1 (`BillPaymentsTest` now checks that a viewer can do none of these). Paying more than is still due is refused by the service unless confirmed. The migration to version 35 makes one payment of each due date already paid with an amount, keeping its date and transaction (`MigrationTest`); a due date paid without an amount counts as paid in full. The tax package counts a Business bill's payments (from both the old paid due dates and the new payments) as business expenses of the bill's person only, in the bill's own group, and only money paid out.
- **Bills from captured documents (BILL-18).** The new bill needs the right to change in the paying account's group, the document is attached with the right to add in its own; a document in a private group linked to a bill in a shared one stays unreadable to those who cannot open its group.
- **The document viewer and itemizing (DOC-01, DOC-02).** One page is drawn at a time, from the decrypted document in memory; nothing is written to disk. Itemizing only computes shares and creates the transaction's split lines through the transaction service, which checks the account's group.
- **Copy details for support (HLP-02).** The version, the operating system's name, version and architecture, Java's version and vendor, the database versions, text size, colours and language: no user or household name, no folder or file path, no amount (`AboutDetailsTest`). The support email link is a fixed `mailto:` address with nothing added; the other links are fixed addresses of RANN's public pages.
- **Walk-Me guides (HLP-03, HLP-04).** Bundled resources read from the class path by name from the bundled contents list; a step's screen, tab, target and condition are only compared with the app's own names, so nothing in a guide is run or opened beyond the manual's own pages. Their text is shown through the manual's Markdown subset. `WalkMeGuidesTest` checks every guide at build time.
- **Column headings (NAV-04)** change only how lists are shown.

## Accepted, with reasons

- **The geocoder gets the exact fix.** Rounding it would give the wrong address; the user turns the lookup on, taps for each address, and the policy says Google receives the coordinates.
- **The full export (EXP-01) holds the bills' account numbers,** as it holds full bank account numbers: it is everything the user can see, saved only where the user chooses, with the warning that it is not encrypted.
- **A meter or place in another group.** Linking a bill to a meter, or naming a saved place in a trip, copies the bill's name and statement number, or the place's name and address, into that record's group: the user chose both and can open both.
- **A phone can link its own photo to any trip id it names.** Ids are random; a photo linked to a trip of a group the phone's user cannot see shows nowhere for them, and others see it only if they can open the photo's group.
- **Android 12 and earlier** still show the last screen in the recent apps list (finding 7): blocking it there would also block every screenshot, which the user may want.
- **Business bill payments re-attributed.** Changing a Business bill's person moves its past payments to the new person in the year-end package; this is the bill's meaning (BILL-20), and the package is reviewed before it is used.

## Tests

New or extended: `TripStopsOnPhoneTest` (photos kept with a private car's group, stop ids sent twice or another trip's), `UntrustedFilesTest` (a page 200 inches square, the resolution cap, EXIF offsets outside the file), `AiTypesTest` (numbers with huge exponents, more than 60 instalments), `BillInstalmentsTest` (more than 60 instalments refused), `BillPaymentsTest` (a viewer can neither pay nor record nor change), `NearbyStationsTest` (hostile answers, size and time limits), `PhoneLockTest` (the own-screen rule after a permission request or a failed launch), `AuditPrivacyTest` (this round's records).
